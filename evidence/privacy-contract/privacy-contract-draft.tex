\begin{abstract}
% TODO(geo): trim to 200 words at submission.
Split training delegates the middle of an LLM to an untrusted cloud and
defends the boundary empirically; the privacy claim has so far been
``the attacker finds nothing.''  We replace that narrative with a
\emph{certified, explicit leakage budget}: the encoder is trained under
$\Ihat(z;x) \le B$ bits/token, estimated by a MINE Donsker--Varadhan
statistics network and enforced by a Lagrangian dual controller, so
privacy becomes a number a customer can write into a contract.  On the
full defense the controller certifies every tier $B \in
\{0.25, 0.5, 1, 2\}$ bits/token at the estimator floor, with the frozen
nine-arm attacker at or below the label-free majority control; the
result transfers to 12B and 35B backbones at $\approx 1\times$ eval
cost.  On a deliberately weakened defense the same controller
\emph{binds}: it converges to the penalty that holds the leakage at the
budget, with monotone pressure across the range.  A second,
disjoint estimator family (Fano/CE probe) corroborates the certified
readings, and K=3 Byzantine replication on CPU-only nodes preserves
integrity at 100\% detection / 0\% false positives over 2{,}256 frames
at a certified tier.  We state the limits plainly: the certificate is a
lower-bound statement in the estimator's terms, and all training
evidence is diagnostic scale.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Split training promises cloud-scale compute for LLM adaptation without
surrendering the data: the client keeps the embedding and the tail, the
cloud runs only a latent middle.  The privacy claim, however, has
remained empirical — ``the attacker finds nothing'' — and empiricism is
not a contracting primitive.  A procurement team cannot write ``trust us,
our probes fail'' into an SLA; an insurer cannot price it; a regulator
cannot audit it.

This paper replaces the narrative with a number.  We train the
client-side encoder under an explicit, enforced leakage budget
$\Ihat(z;x) \le B$ bits per token, where $\Ihat$ is a MINE
Donsker--Varadhan estimate of the mutual information between the released
latent and the token identity, and $B$ is enforced during training by a
Lagrangian dual controller (\S\ref{sec:method}).  Privacy becomes a
quantity that is \emph{certified per training run}, reported in the
artifact, and checkable by a second party who reruns the cell.

Three results carry the paper.  First, the budget is real: on the full
defense, every tier $B \in \{0.25, 0.5, 1, 2\}$ certifies at the
estimator floor with the frozen nine-arm attacker at or below the
label-free majority control, at flat utility cost
(\S\ref{sec:sweep}).  Second, the mechanism is a controller, not a
placebo: on a weakened boundary the dual variable rises to meet the
budget ($\lambda = 2.45$ at $B{=}0.10$) and the tightened run then passes
the attacker gate it would otherwise fail (\S\ref{sec:sweep},
finding~2).  Third, the certification composes with scale and integrity:
12B/35B backbones certify at $\approx 1\times$ eval cost
(\S\ref{sec:transfer}), and $K{=}3$ Byzantine replication on CPU-only
nodes preserves integrity at $100\%$/$0\%$ over full-length runs
(\S\ref{sec:integrity}).

We are equally explicit about what is \emph{not} claimed
(\S\ref{sec:limits}): the certificate is a lower-bound statement in the
estimator's terms, and the training evidence is diagnostic scale.  The
contract we propose is honest about its own coverage.

\section{Threat and System Model}
\label{sec:threat}

\paragraph{Roles.} The client (Toula) is trusted: it holds the corpus,
the frozen embedding and tail blocks, the trainable encoder/decoder pair,
and every gauge secret.  The cloud (Koula) is \emph{fully compromised}:
it may record every frame it serves, run arbitrary computation on its
history, adapt its strategy to public knowledge of the defense, and
return corrupted results.  Integrity adversaries (lying about the
computation) are covered by \S\ref{sec:formal}c; privacy adversaries
(recording and analyzing frames) are the subject of the budget.

\paragraph{Channel.} The only information that crosses the trust
boundary is the released frame: $D{=}64$-dimensional latent rows plus
the protocol metadata of a binary-WebSocket transport under TLS~1.3 with
a pinned CA.  The cloud never sees hidden-width activations, token ids,
backbone gradients, gauge secrets, or the chaff labels.  Per session the
cloud model is seeded identically across integrity replicas and is
otherwise fresh per channel.

\paragraph{Adversary goals and the correct null.}
The privacy adversary's goal is token-identity recovery from released
frames.  Because the token prior is heavily skewed, raw accuracy is
meaningless; following the companion paper~\cite{ourswan}, every
attacker is scored against a label-free majority control, and the
reported quantity is the \emph{excess} over that control with an
upper-95 confidence correction.  Attackers are trained frozen against
each cell's captured bundle (train/eval wire rows plus labels), with
labels confined to the trusted side.

\paragraph{Gates.} A cell passes when: utility delta $\le +0.35$ nats
against the backbone baseline; frozen-attacker excess $\le +1.0$\,pp;
eval-time ratio $\le 3\times$; the cloud state is latent-only; and (new
in this work) the held-out MINE reading certifies the budget,
$\Ihat \le B$.  All gates are computed inside the runner and reported in
the artifact JSON, so the contract claim and the evidence are the same
file.

\paragraph{Certification stance.} The party that wants the certificate
runs the estimator (honest-but-curious certification); the cloud is the
adversary the certificate protects against.  The certificate covers the
run that produced it — it is a per-training-run artifact, not a property
claimed of the architecture in the abstract.

\section{The MINE-Formalized Encoder}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Boundary and release pipeline.}
The frozen backbone is split after layer~21 (0.6B; layer~31 of~36 for the
12B/35B cells).  A client-side encoder compresses the hidden state to a
$D{=}64$ latent row per token position.  Before any row leaves the client,
the release pipeline applies, per frame: (i) per-token Gaussian noise with
clipped norm ($\sigma{=}0.35$, clip $1.0$); (ii) a fresh Haar-random
orthogonal rotation and a fresh token-order permutation, both drawn from a
client-only CSPRNG; and (iii) $48$ chaff rows recycled from earlier blocks
with honestly tracked labels.  The cloud is a radial monomial MoE
($E{=}8$, $L{=}2$) that is \emph{gauge-equivariant} by construction: it
consumes the rotated, permuted, chaffed frame and its output is restored
client-side by inverting the gauges.  Transport is a TLS~1.3
binary-WebSocket protocol with a pinned CA.

\paragraph{Estimator.}
Following MINE~\cite{mine}, a statistics network $T(z, x)$ — a learned
token embedding concatenated with the latent row, followed by a two-layer
GELU MLP — lower-bounds the mutual information between the released row
and the token identity via the Donsker--Varadhan dual:
$\Ihat(z;x) = \mathbb{E}_{\text{joint}}[T] - \log
\mathbb{E}_{\text{marg}}[e^{T}]$, with the marginal term computed on
within-batch row-permuted pairings.  The estimator is trained by gradient
ascent interleaved with the defender's updates, so the penalty is always
evaluated against the estimator's current best view.

\paragraph{Budget enforcement.}
The budget $B$ (bits/token) is enforced by a Lagrangian dual controller
rather than a fixed penalty weight.  Writing the DV bound in nats, after a
warm-up phase the defender updates
\begin{equation}
\lambda \leftarrow \max\!\bigl(0,\; \lambda + \eta\,(\overline{\Ihat} - B\ln 2)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\overline{\Ihat}$ is an exponential moving average (factor $0.05$)
of the per-step DV reading and $\eta = 0.02$.  The defender minimizes
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{distill}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{lang}} +
\mathcal{L}_{\text{adv}} + \lambda\,\Ihat$,
so $\lambda$ converges to the \emph{lightest} penalty that holds the
estimated leakage at or below the budget — and to $\lambda{=}0$ when the
defense already satisfies it, which is exactly what makes the
unconstrained endpoint a measurable control rather than an assumption.

\paragraph{Certificate read-out.}
After training, the estimator is evaluated on held-out eval blocks
(\texttt{mine\_\allowbreak mi\_\allowbreak bits\_\allowbreak held\_\allowbreak out}); a cell certifies when this reading
is at or below $B$ (the \texttt{mine\_budget\_satisfied} gate).  A
measurement-only mode trains the estimator without applying any penalty,
giving the unconstrained endpoint a certified reading as well.

\section{The Formal Stack}
\label{sec:formal}

The certificate rests on three layers, each covering what the previous
one cannot see.

\paragraph{(a) Gauge invariance (provable).}
\begin{proposition}[sketch]
Let the released frame be $\tilde z = Q\,z_{\pi}$ with $Q$ drawn fresh per
frame from the Haar measure on $O(D)$ and $\pi$ a fresh uniform
permutation of the frame's rows.  Then the posterior over $(Q,\pi)$ given
$\tilde z$ is uniform, and any statistic of $\tilde z$ that is not
invariant under the group action is distributionally independent of the
token content $x$.  \emph{Proof sketch:} the group acts freely and
transitively on orientations (resp.\ orders), and Haar measure is the
unique invariant measure; conditioning on $\tilde z$ therefore leaves the
uniform distribution.  The residual channel available to the cloud is
exactly the gauge-invariant content: row norms and the sorted Gram
profile.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{(b) Certified budget on the invariant channel (measured).}
The MINE-certified budget of \S\ref{sec:method} covers what the gauges
cannot: the gauge-invariant residual channel.  We are explicit that this
is a \emph{lower-bound} certificate in the estimator's terms —
$\Ihat_{\mathrm{DV}} \le I(z;x)$ always — so the sellable claim is
``certified at $B$ in this estimator's terms, on a boundary whose
orientation and order channels are provably closed,'' not an
information-theoretic theorem.  \S\ref{sec:robust} strengthens the claim
two ways: the estimator is shown to have dynamic range on a weakened
boundary (it \emph{can} see signal), and a disjoint second estimator
family (Fano/CE probe) corroborates the floor reading on the defended
one.

\paragraph{(c) Byzantine median for integrity (deterministic).}
$K{=}3$ cloud replicas are seeded identically and receive identical
frames and gradients; the client combines restored returns by median and
flags any frame whose per-replica deviation from the group mean exceeds a
calibrated threshold ($0.02$ relative).  One adversarial replica is
therefore both detected and overruled; \S\ref{sec:integrity} measures
$100\%$ detection at $0\%$ false positives over $2{,}256$ frames on
CPU-only nodes.

\section{The Budget Sweep: a Measured Frontier}
\label{sec:sweep}

Table~\ref{tab:sweep} reports the full sweep on the v9.2 winner base and
Fig.~\ref{fig:frontier} visualizes it.  Three findings matter.

\paragraph{1. Every tier certifies at the estimator floor.}
On the full defense, the held-out MINE reading is $\approx 0$ bits/token
at \emph{every} budget tier including the unconstrained endpoint
($-2.1\!\times\!10^{-4}$ bits), so the dual variable never engages
($\lambda = 0$ throughout) and the utility cost is flat
($+0.056$\,\ldots\,$+0.079$ nats, $1.8$–$1.9\times$ eval).  The
implication for the contract is direct: on this boundary, the tightest
tier $B{=}0.25$ is already satisfied with margin, and ``choosing a tier''
is a business decision, not a privacy/utility trade-off.

\paragraph{2. The controller binds when there is leakage to suppress.}
On a deliberately weakened defense (noise $0.15$, no chaff, no gauges)
the same mechanism behaves as a controller should: $\lambda$ converges to
$2.45$ at $B{=}0.10$, to $0.051$ at $B{=}0.25$, and stays at $0$ for
$B \ge 0.50$ — monotone pressure across the range
(Fig.~\ref{fig:frontier}b).  Crucially, the tight-budget cell also passes
the \emph{frozen-attacker} gate (upper-95 excess $+0.67$\,pp $< +1.0$):
at high pressure the MI penalty suppresses probe-visible leakage, not
merely the estimator's own reading.  The binding cell is therefore a
genuine enforcement demonstration, not a curve-fitting artifact.

\paragraph{3. The floor reading is the defense, not the instrument.}
With the estimator strengthened $4\times$ (hidden 512, token embedding
128, 4 estimator updates per round), the weakened boundary reads a
clearly positive $\approx 0.26$ bits/token in the training EMA, while the
full defense still reads $\approx 0$.  The estimator demonstrably
\emph{can} see leakage through this boundary; it sees none on the
defended one.

\paragraph{Long horizon.}
At $10{,}000$ steps the standard tier still certifies (held-out MI
$-6.2\!\times\!10^{-4}$ bits, $\lambda=0$, upper-95 excess $+0.62$\,pp),
and the utility delta \emph{improves} to $+0.026$ nats at $1.79\times$ —
the certificate is stable in the training length, and longer schedules
help utility rather than hurting privacy.

\section{Robustness at a Certified Tier}
\label{sec:robust}

We attempt to falsify the certified standard tier ($B{=}0.50$, seed~42)
with the strongest battery available to us.

\paragraph{Max-effort probe.}
The frozen latent probe re-optimized at $4\times$ its standard budget
($60$ epochs, $8$ restarts, three probe families) reaches $5.16\%$
top-1 — \emph{below} both its own standard-budget reading ($5.42\%$) and
the label-free majority floor ($5.9\%$).  Additional attacker compute
does not move the reading.

\paragraph{Matching and compromise arms.}
Vector-moment-anchor centroid matching recovers $0.028\%$ and
sorted-signature matching $1.35\%$ against the $5.92\%$ majority control;
the capture-compromise battery is flat at $\approx 1.0\%$ top-1 at every
capture fraction from $10\%$ to $100\%$.

\paragraph{Second estimator family.}
A Fano-style CE probe ($I \ge H(\text{tokens}) - \mathrm{CE}$), trained on
the identical released view, reports a vacuous lower bound ($\le 0$ bits,
top-1 $2.07\%$) — corroborating the MINE floor with a disjoint
instrument.

\paragraph{Seeds.}
Re-scoring the standard tier and the unconstrained endpoint on seeds
$43$/$44$ reproduces seed~$42$ on every metric (MI within
$10^{-3}$ bits, excess $-0.20$\,\ldots\,$-0.51$\,pp, utility
$+0.061$\,\ldots\,$+0.071$).

\section{Integrity Under a Certified Budget}
\label{sec:integrity}

With $K{=}3$ CPU-only replicas (soula/voula/sasa) under the median
combine of \S\ref{sec:formal}c at the certified $B{=}0.50$ tier, the
full-length ($2{,}000$-step) honest run flags $0$ of $2{,}256$ frames
(max relative deviation $4.9\!\times\!10^{-8}$), and a single replica
applying an undeclared $0.5$ perturbation is caught at $2{,}256/2{,}256$
frames ($100\%$, max deviation $0.59$) with utility intact.  The
$9$–$10\times$ eval-time overhead on these nodes is weak hardware, not
the mechanism: the same cell on the GPU pair runs at $1.85\times$.

\section{Business and Product Implications}
\label{sec:business}

The budget is the product.  (1)~\emph{Contractible privacy}: $B$ is a
signable number with a machine-checkable gate
(\texttt{mine\_budget\_satisfied}) in the run artifact — a customer
audits the JSON, not the marketing.  (2)~\emph{Product tiers}: the
sweep's flat utility cost means strict ($0.25$), standard ($0.50$), and
performance ($\ge 1.0$) tiers can be priced on contract terms rather than
on a measured utility trade-off — unusual, and simpler to sell.
(3)~\emph{Insurable risk}: a per-run certified bound with a frozen
falsification battery is actuarial input.  (4)~\emph{Short procurement}:
every number in this paper regenerates from committed scripts on
documented hardware, so a second party can clear the reproducibility bar
in days.  The caveat we keep in the sales language mirrors
\S\ref{sec:limits}: the claim is ``certified in this estimator's terms,
on a provably gauge-closed boundary, with integrity,'' not a theorem.

\section{Transfer}
\label{sec:transfer}

The certified tier is not a small-model artifact.  On the 35B hybrid-MoE
backbone, both $B{=}0.25$ and $B{=}0.50$ certify at the estimator floor
with the frozen attacker $0.58$–$0.72$\,pp \emph{below} the majority
floor and utility delta $+0.04$ nats at $\approx 1.0\times$ eval cost —
the boundary gets cheaper, not dearer, as the backbone grows.  On the 12B
backbone at $B{=}0.50$, the candidate \emph{beats} the backbone baseline
($-0.411$ nats), reproducing the known 12B utility anomaly of the
companion campaign.  All transfer cells pass the budget gate.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
(1) DV/MINE is a \emph{lower bound}: the certificate is ``$\le B$ in this
estimator's terms.''  We mitigate with a dynamic-range proof
(\S\ref{sec:sweep}, finding~3) and a second estimator family
(\S\ref{sec:robust}), but neither constitutes an upper bound.
(2) Diagnostic scale: $2{,}000$-step runs on a $16{,}896$-token corpus
window; one $10{,}000$-step cell at the standard tier confirms stability
of the certificate (\S\ref{sec:sweep}) but not full training
convergence.
(3) At very tight budgets the controller needs longer schedules: at
$B{=}0.10$ on the weakened defense the training EMA ($0.205$ bits) had
not fully converged to budget within $2{,}000$ steps even under
$\lambda = 2.45$, although the held-out certificate passed.
(4) The estimator can under-read relative to probe recovery: on weakened
cells the probe's upper-95 excess reaches $+2.6$\,pp where MINE reads
$\approx 0.26$ bits.  The two measure different quantities (a lower bound
in bits vs.\ a point recovery margin), but the gap bounds how much
certification strength we claim.
(5) Single corpus (WikiText-2), single threat configuration per cell.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\emph{MI-bounded privacy adapters.} The UCLA information-bottleneck
adapters~\cite{uclaib} formalize MI budgets for inference-time adapters
but never measure a floor against an adaptive attacker and carry no
gauges, chaff, or integrity story; the Fraunhofer SAE dual-channel
construction~\cite{fraunhofersparse} derives closed-form MI budgets in
vision, the closest analog to our budget in the LLM setting.
\emph{Obfuscation systems}~\cite{aloepri, gelo, obfnet, sap, pidi}
report empirical attacker failure without a certified number.
\emph{Hardware trust} (TEE)~\cite{phalatee, ethtee, fastrack} achieves
$<$7--20\% overhead but assumes a trusted enclave — the assumption we
deliberately exclude.  \emph{MPC/HE}~\cite{sigma, bumblebee, nexus,
nimbus, puma, zkllm, zkgpt} gives cryptographic guarantees at 44\,s to
14\,min per token, two to four orders of magnitude over plaintext: the
cost argument for empirical obfuscation.  \emph{Integrity:}
TOPLOC~\cite{toploc} is the post-hoc analog of our online $K{=}3$ vote;
PermLLM and shuffling
analyses~\cite{permllm, shufflinginsecurity, permeq, permreversal}
motivate the permutation gauge and our matching-attack battery.
\emph{Estimator:} MINE~\cite{mine}; DP-SGD accounting~\cite{dp}.
The companion paper~\cite{ourswan} establishes the empirical defense
this work certifies.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Split LLM training on compromised nodes no longer has to ask for trust.
An explicit leakage budget — estimated by MINE, enforced by a dual
controller, corroborated by a second estimator family, and surviving our
strongest attacks — turns privacy into a contractible engineering
quantity, at flat utility cost, on backbones up to 35B, with Byzantine
integrity on commodity CPU witnesses.  The open theoretical work is a
true upper bound; the open engineering work is longer schedules for
sub-$0.1$-bit tiers.  Both are stated, with the evidence, so the reader
can see exactly where the certificate ends.

\section{Proof of the Gauge-Invariance Proposition}
\label{app:proof}

\emph{Setup.} A frame is a set of latent rows $z = \{z_1,\dots,z_T\}
\subset \mathbb{R}^D$ produced by the client encoder from token content
$x$.  Release applies $\tilde z = s \cdot Q z_{\pi}$ where $Q$ is drawn
fresh per frame from the Haar measure $\mu$ on $O(D)$, $\pi$ is a fresh
uniform permutation of the row index set, and $s$ (when the scale gauge
is enabled) is an independent log-normal scalar.  The triple
$\sigma = (Q, \pi, s)$ is drawn from the product measure and is known
only to the client.

\emph{Claim.} For any measurable set $A$ of released frames and any two
contents $x, x'$ whose ungauged frames lie in the same orbit of the
action,
$\Pr[\tilde z \in A \mid x] = \Pr[\tilde z \in A \mid x']$.

\emph{Proof.}  The group $G = O(D) \times S_T \times \mathbb{R}_{>0}$
acts on frames by $(Q,\pi,s) \cdot z = s\,Q z_\pi$.  The action is
free (a frame has trivial stabilizer a.s., since rows are distinct a.s.)
and the pushforward of the product Haar measure under the orbit map
$g \mapsto g \cdot z$ is the unique $G$-invariant measure on the orbit
$G \cdot z$, independent of which point $z$ of the orbit we started
from.  Since $\tilde z$ is by construction distributed according to this
invariant measure on the orbit of the content frame, its law depends on
$x$ only through the orbit.  Orientation-, order-, and scale-dependent
statistics of $\tilde z$ therefore carry no information about $x$;
equivalently, the conditional distribution of $\tilde z$ given the orbit
is uniform, and the residual channel is exactly the orbit's invariant
content: row norms and the sorted Gram profile (both preserved by $Q$,
$\pi$, and, after radial normalization, by $s$). \hfill$\square$

\emph{Remark (what the proposition does not cover).}  The invariant
content itself \emph{can} depend on $x$ — that is precisely the channel
the MINE-certified budget of \S\ref{sec:method} bounds, and the two
layers compose because every released frame factors through
$\tilde z = \sigma \cdot z$: invariance kills the non-invariant part,
the budget bounds the invariant part.

\section{Reproduction Recipe}
\label{app:repro}

\emph{Hardware.} Two NVIDIA GB10 (aarch64) GPU nodes (toula: client and
attacker training; koula: latent cloud server, \texttt{wss://koula:5025})
and three 4-core ARM CPU nodes (soula/voula/sasa, 192.168.1.120--122) as
Byzantine replicas, each serving \texttt{latent\_cloud\_\allowbreak server.py} on
port~5013 with per-node certs from the pinned experiment CA.

\emph{Cells.} All cells run in the \texttt{split-inference:spark}
container on toula against the frozen local models
\texttt{qwen3-0.6b}, \texttt{mistral-nemo-12b-\allowbreak instruct},
and \texttt{qwen36-35b-a3b}, and the committed WikiText-2 corpus window:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\footnotesize
\item budget sweep: \texttt{bin/latent\_\allowbreak v14\_\allowbreak stage.sh}
\item seed re-score: \texttt{bin/latent\_\allowbreak v14\_\allowbreak seeds.sh}
\item transfer (12B/35B): \texttt{bin/latent\_\allowbreak v14\_\allowbreak transfer.sh}
\item max-effort battery + CE cross-check:
      \texttt{bin/latent\_\allowbreak v14\_\allowbreak maxeffort.sh}
\item integrity (honest/malicious):
      \texttt{bin/latent\_\allowbreak v14\_\allowbreak byzantine\_\allowbreak stage.sh <cell>}
      (400 steps; \texttt{STEPS=2000} for full length)
\item estimator-power / binding range: \texttt{bin/latent\_\allowbreak v15\_\allowbreak stage.sh},
      \texttt{bin/latent\_\allowbreak v15b\_\allowbreak stage.sh}
\item long horizon: \texttt{bin/latent\_\allowbreak v14\_\allowbreak longhaul.sh}
\end{itemize}
Attacker bundles are deleted after scoring by policy; the ledger
(Appendix~\ref{app:ledger}) regenerates with
\texttt{python3 bin/make\_evidence\_appendix.py}.
