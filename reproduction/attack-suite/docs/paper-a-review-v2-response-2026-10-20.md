The main result is a controlled comparison at a fixed trained checkpoint, candidate count and beam width.

The primary recorded token gain at $k{=}32$, beam width two remains 3.17 percentage points (`94.20%` to `97.38%`), with original 95% interval [2.72, 3.64]. The post-review expansions clarify how this gradient benefit and the supporting placement findings behave across search budgets, live training traffic, defended splits, longer passages, modern GQA/RoPE/SwiGLU architectures, and fixed-boundary interventions:

1. **Candidate budget vs. beam width (`Section 4.7`, `Table 8`)**: Widening the beam at $k{=}32$ to four or eight leaves the paired gradient gain at `+3.17 pp` (`[+2.55, +3.86]`) and `+3.27 pp` (`[+2.61, +3.98]`), whereas expanding the candidate list at beam width two on GPT-2 raises activation-only recovery to `96.82%` at $k{=}64$ (paired gain `+0.92 pp`, `[+0.52, +1.34]`) and `98.10%` at $k{=}128$ versus `98.16%` with gradients (paired gain `+0.06 pp`, 10-contrast Bonferroni interval `[-0.15, +0.27]`; exact passages `49.02%` versus `49.92%`, at `3.46×` online search time), so no clear gradient advantage is established at $k{=}128$ on GPT-2.

### Exact method clarification for R5.2

Use zero-based positions and define `N(x) = x / max(||x||_2, 1e-12)`. With observed seam gradient `g_t`, attacker-held token embedding `e_v`, and calibration-majority token `m`, the executed backward score is:

```text
b_t(v) = N(-g_(t-1))^T N(e_v),  for t >= 1
b_0(v) = 1[v = m]
```

The ridge map is used only by the activation score `a_t(v) = N(h_t W)^T N(e_v)`. There is no backward ridge fit. GPT-2's seam and embedding widths coincide; the backward heuristic compares these coordinates directly. Equal width does not provide a chain-rule justification, and the paper now states this limitation explicitly.

Backward-only emits `argmax_v b_t(v)` independently at each position, including the majority token at position zero, without ridge decoding or beam search. Joint decoding uses the stable unique concatenation of the forward top-k/2, backward top-k/2 and the full forward top-k list, truncated to k IDs. Position zero uses the full forward list. Thus overlap refill can restore more than k/2 forward-derived candidates. The common width-two beam ranks cumulative squared activation residual; the gradient changes proposals, not that objective. Later beam choices can indirectly change the selected first token even though its candidate list has no previous-position gradient suggestion.

These rules are in `attacker/reconstruction_baselines.py:74–144` at frozen commit `064a64f749be2f82a770f4782589b621b086956d`. The added paper equations document that algorithm; they do not claim a faithful implementation of full TAG or a new theoretical gradient-to-embedding identity.
