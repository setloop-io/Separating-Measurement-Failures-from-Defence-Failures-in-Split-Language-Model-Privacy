## 1. Executive Diagnosis: Why the Paper Derailed to 61 Pages

Across six rounds of review (`review-what-gradients-add-*.pdf`, 18 review reports total in [reviews-indepedent/](file:///Users/geo/LLM-Attacker/reviews-indepedent)), three distinct reviewer archetypes evaluated the manuscript:
1. **Reviewer Archetype A (`PoPETs`)** — *Systems Security & Privacy Program Committee perspective* (`review-3`, `6`, `9`, `12`, `15`, `18`).
2. **Reviewer Archetype B (`xPeerd — DAReview`)** — *Methodology, Claim Calibration & External Validity perspective* (`review-1`, `4`, `7`, `10`, `13`, `16`).
3. **Reviewer Archetype C (`reviewer3.com`)** — *Formal/Numerical Audit & Experimental Confound perspective* (`review-2`, `5`, `8`, `11`, `14`, `17`).

### Why Previous Edits Made the Paper Worse
The initial baseline paper (`arXiv:2610.04128`, 26 pages total: ~14 pages of main body + references and appendices) had a clear, disciplined structure and received constructive reviews. In subsequent rounds, instead of integrating new experimental evidence into the existing structure and keeping dense tables in the Appendix, **each review comment was answered by pasting new subsections, multi-page tables, and defensive caveats directly into the main text**:
- **Main-body table bloat**: Nine large tables (`Table 1`, `Table 7`, `Table 8`, `Table 9`, `Table 10`, `Table 11`, `Table 12`, `Table 13`, plus coupled float blocks) were inserted into the main text, turning Sections 1 and 4 into a 32-page wall of numbers.
- **Abstract & Conclusion overload**: Every time a reviewer asked for a nuance, another clause and number were appended to the Abstract (~60 numbers in one paragraph) and Conclusion (a single 350-word paragraph).
- **Reactive numeric drift**: Adding ad-hoc prose descriptions of tables without a single automated query index caused rounding mismatches (`53.58%` FP32 vs `53.56%` FP64 = `-0.02 pp` displayed vs `-0.01 pp` unrounded) and stale cross-references (`b = 1, 4, 7` in §5 vs `b_train in {0, 2, 4, 6}` in Table 21; `220.8 s` vs `220.29 s`).
- **Scope blurring**: Companion-framework single-seam baselines (`Learned SIP`, `BRE-bf`) and third-party activations-only methods (`SipIt`) were mixed into the main narrative alongside our core contribution, confusing reviewers about what is new in *this* paper versus what is a comparison baseline.

## 3. Clear Separation of Scope: Our Work vs. Third-Party & Companion Comparisons

A central requirement for restructuring the paper is to draw an unmistakable boundary between **this paper's scope and contribution** and **third-party or companion baselines**:

1. **Our Work (Core Scope of This Paper)**:
   - **Research Question**: In split language model training, how much *additional* text does a passive observer recover from backward cut-layer gradients beyond what forward cut-layer activations already reveal under a matched search budget, and how does that answer change across candidate budgets ($k$), beam widths ($B$), cut depths ($L$), passage lengths ($T$), calibration set sizes ($C$), live training trajectories, architectures (GPT-2 and Qwen3-0.6B), and cut-layer defences?
   - **Controlled Attribution Apparatus**: A shared, lightweight ridge-regression + activation-residual beam decoder where the *only* experimental toggle between `forward` and `joint_true` is replacing $k/2$ ridge-proposed candidates at positions $t \ge 1$ with $k/2$ shifted negative-gradient candidates, holding candidate budget $k$, beam width $B$, calibration data, and forward-pass verification work strictly equal (`joint_shuffled` breaks position alignment as a negative control).
   - **Regime-Dependent Findings**:
     1. *Compute shortcut vs. information advantage*: On GPT-2 at $L{=}6$, gradient proposals act as a $3.46\times$ compute shortcut at $k{=}32$ ($+3.17\text{ pp}$ token, $+24.06\text{ pp}$ exact passage), because widening activation-only search to $k{=}128$ closes the token gap to $+0.06\text{ pp}$ (`98.10%` vs `98.16%`). Conversely, at deeper cuts ($L{=}11$ on GPT-2: $+3.43\text{ pp}$ at $k{=}128$) and on Qwen3-0.6B ($L{=}7, 14, 21$: $+2.87\text{ pp}$ to $+21.37\text{ pp}$ at $k{=}32$, and $+14.18\text{ pp}$ / $+14.93\text{ pp}$ at $L{=}14, k{=}128$), widening $k$ or calibration size $C$ up to $384$ does *not* close the gap.
     2. *Token vs. exact-passage divergence & the position-0 selection mechanism*: Token accuracy and exact-passage accuracy diverge sharply across passage length $T \in \{32, 64, 128\}$, live training traffic (`+6.15 pp` token vs `2.40%` exact passages), and Qwen3 public-grant cuts (`82.35%` token vs `0.00%` exact passages at $L{=}21$). The exact-passage ceiling is governed by **position-0 activation-residual selection under prefix drift** (where $C_0 = F_0$ has no backward gradient cross-check: true-token recall in $F_0$ is `83.67%` on GPT-2 $L{=}6$ and `49.12%–50.73%` on Qwen3 $L{\in}\{14,21\}$ public, but beam selection is only `52.97%/53.87%` and `0.39%–0.54%`).

2. **Third-Party & Companion Comparisons (External Context, Not Claimed as Novel Attacks Here)**:
   - **Third-Party Activation-Only Guarantees (`SipIt`, Rossi et al. 2025)**: Positioned in Related Work (§2) and Discussion (§5) as the formal activation-only invertibility baseline that motivates our question (since activation-only recoverability is already known in principle, the open question is what backward gradients add under bounded search).
   - **Companion Single-Seam Baselines (`Learned SIP` GRU inverter after Wan et al. 2025; `BRE-bf` continuous embedding relaxation after Gupta et al. 2022; and the multi-seam `BiSR` framework in our companion paper)**: Confined to a dedicated, compact **External Baseline Comparison** subsection (§4.6, with full tables in Appendix G) explicitly labeled as single-seam reference implementations adapted from the literature to benchmark our ridge+beam attribution apparatus under the identical 32-document / 15,604-window observation contract.

## 5. Target IEEE S&P / PoPETs Paper Structure (Restored to ~15 Main Pages + Structured Appendices)

Using `arXiv:2610.04128` (`head_main.tex`) as our structural baseline, the restructured manuscript is organized into **6 concise main sections (~14–15 pages)** followed by **References** and **10 self-contained Appendices**:

### Main Body (~14–15 Pages)
- **Title & Abstract (~180 words)**: Crisp problem statement, factorial attribution method, and the 3 headline contrasts (GPT-2 $L{=}6$ primary test vs. $k{=}128$ compute-shortcut closure; live training token vs. exact-passage divergence; Qwen3-0.6B depth transition).
- **§1 Introduction (~2 pages)**:
  - The attribution question in split LLM training: forward activations vs. backward cut-layer gradients at matched search budget.
  - Why single-metric or unmatched evaluations mislead (compute shortcuts vs. information advantages; token vs. exact-passage divergence; position-0 selection bottleneck).
  - Scope boundary: our controlled attribution factorial vs. third-party (`SipIt`) and companion (`Learned SIP`, `BRE-bf`, `BiSR`) baselines.
  - Compact 4-bullet Contributions list with pointers to the experimental sections and Appendix claim map.
- **§2 Background and Related Work (~1.5 pages)**:
  - Split learning wire boundary and notation.
  - Prior work on activation inversion (`SipIt`, `SIP`, `UnSplit`, `Qiu et al.`), gradient inversion (`TAG`, `LAMP`, `FILM`, `Decepticons`, `DAGER`, `Du et al. SoK`), and cut-layer defences (`NoPeek`, `InstaHide`, label leakage).
- **§3 Threat Model, Attribution Decoder, and Experimental Protocol (~3 pages)**:
  - **§3.1 Passive Wire-Observer Threat Model & Access-Grant Taxonomy**: **Table 1 (Access-Grant & Campaign Taxonomy)** — compact main-body table declaring victim model, cut depth, client weight grant (`public` vs. `trained`), calibration budget $C$, and evaluation tier (`Tier 1 Confirmatory`, `Tier 2 Planned Sweeps`, `Tier 3 Post-hoc`).
  - **§3.2 Shared Attribution Decoder**: Exact equations with consistent dimensions ($E, U \in \mathbb{R}^{V \times d}$, $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times d}$) and zero-based indexing $t \in \{0,\dots,T{-}1\}$:
    - Eq. (1)–(2): Ridge activation-to-embedding map $W$ and forward candidate list $F_t$.
    - Eq. (3): Position-0 candidate set $C_0 = F_0$ and 1-token activation-residual scoring $\mathcal{L}(v)$.
    - Eq. (4): Shifted negative-gradient candidate list $B_t$ for $t \ge 1$ and 50/50 deduplicated union $C_t$.
    - Eq. (5): Width-$B$ prefix activation-residual beam search and exact work equivalence.
  - **§3.3 Metrics & Statistical Protocol**: Token accuracy, position-0 vs. positions 1–31 accuracy, contiguous span recovery $\text{Span}_{\ge 8}$, exact-passage recovery $E_d = \mathbb{I}[M_d = T]$, hierarchical bootstrap over victim seeds and passages, and pre-specified primary pass mark ($+2.0\text{ pp}$, $p_{\text{boot}} < 0.01$).
- **§4 Experimental Evaluation (~6.5 pages)**:
  - **§4.1 Primary Confirmatory Test (GPT-2, $L{=}6, k{=}32, B{=}2$)**: **Table 2 (Primary Fixed-Checkpoint Contrast)** and **Figure 1 (Primary Token vs. Exact-Passage Recovery)**. Decomposes candidate recall (`97.97%` vs `99.18%`), beam selection efficiency (`96.15%` vs `98.18%`), and the position-0 residual-selection bottleneck (`83.67%` recall in $F_0$ vs `52.97%/53.87%` selection).
  - **§4.2 When Gradients Are a Compute Shortcut vs. Information Advantage (Budget $k$, Beam $B$, Depth $L$, Calibration $C$, Horizon $T$)**: **Figure 2 (Budget & Depth Scaling)** and **Table 3 (Compact Regime Summary across $k$, $L$, $C$, and $T$)**. Shows that at $L{=}6$, widening activation-only search to $k{=}128$ (`98.10%` vs `98.16%`, $+0.06\text{ pp}$) or increasing calibration to $C{=}64$ ($+1.99\text{ pp}$ at $k{=}32$) closes the gap ($3.46\times$ compute shortcut), whereas at deeper cuts ($L{=}11$: $+3.43\text{ pp}$ at $k{=}128$) and longer horizons ($T \in \{64, 128\}$, where exact passage recovery falls `37.92% -> 21.57% -> 7.37%`) the gradient gap persists. Full grids in Appendices B–C.
  - **§4.3 Live Split-Training Traffic & Prefix Drift**: **Figure 3 (Live Training Trajectory)** and inline summary of **Table 4 (Live Trajectory & Client-Grant Drift)**. Contrasts the `+6.15 pp` live token gain (`86.10%` vs `79.96%`) against the collapse of exact-passage recovery (`2.40%` vs `0.33%`), explaining why the `0.61 pp` checkpoint gap reflects the short 128-step fine-tuning horizon.
  - **§4.4 Cut-Layer Defences & Adaptive Counter-Attacks**: **Figure 4 (Defended Regimes)** (full 18-row table in Appendix D). Summarizes unadapted vs. public-grant adapted recovery across `replica-median`, `secret-mixup` (noting first-$B$ position-0 initialization), `trained-bottleneck` (distinguishing realistic public-prefix `adapt_ridge_*` from the white-box `adapt_pinv_*` upper-bound stress test), and `mid-lite`, explicitly framing all defended recovery rates as attack-conditioned empirical lower bounds.
  - **§4.5 Cross-Architecture Generalization: Qwen3-0.6B ($L \in \{1, 7, 14, 21\}$)**: **Table 5 (Compact Qwen3-0.6B Depth & Budget Summary with 8-Seed SDs)** (full depth and calibration tables in Appendix F). Highlights the depth transition from $L{=}1$ (`-0.63 pp` public, `-0.47 pp` trained due to 50/50 slot displacement) to $L{=}7$ (`+2.87 pp` / `+2.02 pp`), $L{=}14$ (`+13.85 pp` / `+14.08 pp` at $k{=}32$; `+14.18 pp` / `+14.93 pp` at $k{=}128$), and $L{=}21$ (`+17.79 pp` / `+21.37 pp` at $k{=}32$, with `forward_k128` candidate recall upper-bounded at `81.47%` public / `82.19%` trained), plus the public-grant position-0 residual-selection failure (`49.12–50.73%` recall in $F_0$ vs `0.39–0.54%` selection).
  - **§4.6 Comparison with Companion Single-Seam Learned and Optimization Baselines**: Compact subsection (full table in Appendix G) comparing our ridge+beam arms against in-domain `Learned SIP` (`15,604` WikiText-2 windows), `Learned SIP-cal32`, and `BRE-bf` (32 gradient steps + 800 activation-refinement steps) under the identical observation contract.
- **§5 Discussion, Scope Boundaries, and Limitations (~1 page)**:
  - Practical guidance for split-learning evaluations (why evaluations must report the access grant, search budget $k$, and both token and sequence-level recovery).
  - Scope boundaries: relationship to `SipIt` (activation-only guarantees) and companion multi-seam `BiSR` (Regime B partial offloading `b_train in {0, 2, 4, 6}`, Appendix H).
  - Limitations: single-benchmark (WikiText-2), $T \le 128$, fixed 50/50 candidate split, short 128-step fine-tuning horizon, and non-universality of empirical bounds.
- **§6 Conclusion (~0.5 pages)**: Three concise paragraphs summarizing the factorial methodology, the regime-dependent findings, and the takeaway for split-learning privacy evaluation.
