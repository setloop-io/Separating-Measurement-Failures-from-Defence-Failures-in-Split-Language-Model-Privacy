**The draft's revised central utility claim broadly agrees with the independent assessment. Its privacy claims still exceed what the implemented tests establish, and several strong statements contradict caveats elsewhere in the same draft.** The evidence ledger already marks the paper `not_submission_ready`, appropriately.

**What is already appropriately qualified.**

The abstract distinguishes historical pre-gradient-protection confirmation from repaired controls. It correctly says a confidence interval containing zero does not establish equivalence, and its central claim is a corpus-slice utility transition without detected trained-remote advantage. The conclusion correctly separates utility, remote necessity and actual work. Keep these distinctions and give the independently trained zero-return result equal prominence with frozen random.

The draft already identifies the invalid 27B nominal percentages and empty tails in its dedicated section, and already treats truncated depths as damaged baselines. These findings are corroborated by the independent review, not newly discovered omissions from that section. The remaining problem is inconsistent wording elsewhere, including the future-work claim above and retaining invalid nominal labels in a main results table. The split75 “control” also needs explicit identification as full-model-plus-adapter execution, not a matched untouched control.

The draft accurately cautions that the quality metric is not standard HellaSwag accuracy, that offline composition bypasses wire defenses, and that energy estimates do not show savings. These caveats should remain.

**Privacy language needs more than another disclaimer.**

The abstract/conclusion now describe naked-versus-defended as an operational contrast rather than a causal suppression ratio. But the contribution list and limitations still advertise a measured reduction multiple, and the table says “bounded residual” and “nothing beyond the wire.” Even consistent attacker-specific language would not repair the target and row-selection issues: those alter what was measured. The strongest supportable statement pending reruns is that the implemented next-token classifiers have lower scores on the defended configurations, with population, width, attack-selection and provenance differences explicitly identified.

The mandatory positive-control principle is sound, but it must validate the intended secret target and the scored observation. The retained naked result does not currently validate input-token reconstruction on exact captured bytes. Its battery artifact has 256 training blocks and 4,096 evaluation blocks; the deep-probe artifact has the same dimensions and no capture binding, consistent with the script's regenerated-bundle path. This is a concrete provenance gap, not proof that no other remote artifact exists.

Evidence for this additional comparison finding: [naked battery artifact](../../paper-data/collected/diagnostic/real_naked/real_naked_split14_attacker.json), [deep probe artifact](../../paper-data/collected/diagnostic/real_naked/real_naked_split14_deepprobe.json), [launch script](../../bin/run_real_naked_test.sh), and [asset generator](../../bin/build_delegation_paper_assets.py), which selects these files.

**Recommended paper verdict:** major revision. Preserve the frozen-model boundary-adaptation and remote-necessity control results. Suspend reconstruction-privacy conclusions until the evaluator is corrected. Resolve contradictory claims and remeasure latency before presenting a private-delegation or systems-efficiency result. No manuscript changes were made as part of this review.
